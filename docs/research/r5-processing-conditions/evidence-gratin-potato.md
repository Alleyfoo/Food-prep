# Evidence: gratin and boiled-potato conditions (pairs 10-13, 24, 25)

Date read: 2026-11-25 (all entries). Method: WebSearch to find pages, then WebFetch to read them.
WebFetch returns a summary written by a model, not the raw page, so numbers and reader comments
are second-hand. Four kuechengoetter.de PDFs and two Swiss PDFs (swisspatat, Betty Bossi) came back as
binary. Their text was extracted locally with `pdftotext` and read as raw text; those entries say
"raw text (pdftotext)". Quotes are at most 15 words and only for endpoint or failure wording.

Search languages: Finnish, Swedish (Sweden and Finland-Swedish), Norwegian, German (Germany and
Switzerland), French, English (UK, Ireland, US), Japanese, Hindi/English (India).

Earlier evidence is cited, not re-read: R4 `r4-transfers/evidence-gratin-stems.md` (G1-G27) and R2
`r2-construction-patterns/recipes.md` (M15a-b gratin dauphinois, E1a-d rösti, A2a-b aloo tikki,
A6a korokke). Five earlier pages were **re-read** because the R5 scope addendum needs endpoint and
dependency wording the earlier notes do not have: G22 = Q231, G11 = Q232, G10 = Q233, G7 = Q234,
E1c = Q236. Those entries say which earlier id they repeat. Do not count them as new independent
sources.

Traps:
- Vocabulary. Swedish *kålrabbi* is kohlrabi, but Swedish *kålrot* and Norwegian *kålrot*/*kålrabi* are
  swede. Swiss *Bodenkohlrabi* is Steckrübe (swede). The Finnish *kermaperunat* is the home potato
  gratin; *lanttulaatikko* is a mashed swede casserole, a different dish and a different endpoint.
- Finnish lanttu-peruna gratin: still not found (R4 did not find one either). Finnish evidence for
  pair 10 therefore comes from other lanttu dishes (contrasting endpoints), and for pair 11 from
  kermaperunat.
- MTV's "Maailman parhaat kermaperunat" (Q200) is a Finnish write-up of a recipe by a US chef (Tyler
  Florence). It is Finnish publication practice, not Finnish tradition.
- The Betty Bossi knife test (Q207) is printed as a general tip for Gschwellti. It sits next to rösti
  recipes, but the page does not say it is the endpoint for rösti potatoes.
- Several search-engine summaries made up "practical" times (for example "blanch 2-3 min",
  "3-5 min for ¼-inch slices"). These are not evidence and are not used.

### Q200 — https://www.mtvuutiset.fi/makuja/reseptit/resepti/maailman-parhaat-kermaperunat-helppo-ohje/7572428
- Finnish, Finland. Magazine (MTV Uutiset Makuja, Petra Tuominen). 2019-10-02. Credited to US chef Tyler Florence. Read: WebFetch summary.

- Completion: "kunnes perunat ovat kypsiä" (until the potatoes are cooked). No test is named. [11]
- Rest: about 20 min before serving, so the dish sets. [13]

### Q201 — https://info.fr/forum/questions-pratiques/gratin-dauphinois-comment-faire-sans-que-ce-soit-un-desastre/
- French, France. User forum, several home cooks. March-April 2026. Read: WebFetch summary.
- Start: thin slices, raw. Two posters say not to pre-cook; one calls pre-cooking "une erreur assez courante". [11, 12]
- Medium and time, by poster: 200 °C 40 min (Siham44); 180 °C 50 min (Ultra-Dude); 200 °C 40 min under foil, then 10 min uncovered (Elena3167); foil for 35-40 min, then uncovered (Beau-Samourai88). Liquid ranges from 250 ml cream + 250 ml milk to 1 l crème fraîche for 2 kg of potato. [11]

### Q202 — https://dubocalalassiette.fr/blog/temps-de-cuisson-gratin-dauphinois-four-chaleur-tournante/
- French, France. Shop blog (bulk-food shop near Rennes). © 2021, no article date. Read: WebFetch summary.
- Start: 3-4 mm slices from a mandoline; firm-fleshed potatoes; not rinsed after slicing. [11, 12]
- Main method: potatoes simmered submerged in milk and cream (time not given), then the oven: 200 °C for 15-20 min, then 160-170 °C fan for **at least 1 h 30**. Without a fan, use the centre rack and turn the dish halfway (occasional work). [12, 11]
- Shifts: says some recipes pre-cook about 10 min in the pan, which shortens the oven time but can lose the creaminess of slow cooking. A lower temperature for longer gives softer potatoes. Ovens differ. [11, 12]
- Completion: top "bien doré en surface" (nicely golden on top). [11]
- Failures and stated causes: watery, from non-firm potatoes or rinsing the slices; raw or not creamy, from too little oven time, "a common mistake"; dry, fixed with a little hot cream poured on, then covered for a few minutes; the milk boiling over during the simmer, so keep it at a simmer. [11, 12]
- Rest: 10 min, so the cream sets. [13]
- Keeps: make 6-24 h ahead; cool, cover and refrigerate overnight for better flavour and easier slicing. Reheat covered at 120 °C for at least 20 min. Another passage on the same page says 150 °C for about 20 min (inconsistent). Hold briefly in a lukewarm oven. If frozen, the cream may separate on thawing. [13]

### Q219 — https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/food_and_recipes/2036754-Dauphinoise-Drama
- English, UK. User forum. 2014-03-26. Read: WebFetch summary.
- Failure reported: raw potatoes assembled with milk (cream gave the same result). It curdled into "flaccid looking slivers in grainy watery liquid". [11]
- Replies:
  - Use cream, not only milk.
  - Part-cook the slices. The poster agreed that not parboiling was her mistake.
  - Do not soak before parboiling: the starch binds the sauce and stops it splitting.
  - Poach the slices in half milk, half cream before baking; or boil them in milk and cream "until just softened", then bake at 160 °C for at least 1.5 h.
  - The opposite method: soak raw slices overnight, cover with double cream and bake at about 120 (unit not stated) for about 3 h.
  - Single cream splits more readily than double cream. [11, 12]

### Q224 — https://indianhealthyrecipes.com/aloo-tikki-recipe/

### Q231 — https://ellerepublic.de/en/potato-rutabaga-gratin/ (re-read of G22)
- English, Germany-based personal blog. © 2026. Read: WebFetch summary, asked for exact endpoint wording.

## Sequence notes

Per the coordinator's scope addendum. The ids are the evidence for each item. Readiness = stopped early because a later step finishes it. Doneness = the final edible state.

### transfer:rutabaga_gratin (potato peel, slice 2 mm; rutabaga peel, slice 2-3 mm, blanch; layer; bake; rest)
1. **Potato: peel, slice.** 2-3 mm [Q231, Q225], 3 mm [Q232, G7]. Do not rinse after slicing, because the starch thickens the sauce [Q235, Q225, Q202]. Slice the potatoes last to avoid oxidation [Q231]. Readiness: raw. Rutabaga recipes leave the potato raw [Q231], or give it 1 min [Q232].
2. **Rutabaga: peel, slice 2-3 mm.** [Q231]; 4 mm with a longer pre-cook [Q232].
3. **Rutabaga: blanch.** Readiness, not doneness: "crisp tender" [Q231]; "will not be fully cooked" [Q232]; "almost tender" at 10 min [G18].
   - Dependency: rutabaga is "a very dense root vegetable" and needs longer than potato [Q231]. Denser roots cook through more slowly than potato [Q234].
   - Skipped: raw thin slices stay crunchy after the stated bake [G20, G21].
   - Overdone: not found. Q231 only says 2-3 min more gives a softer result.
4. **Layer.** Potato on the top and bottom layers [G7/Q234, G15]. Flour (3 tbsp) because the salted roots release moisture [Q231].
5. **Bake (post-assembly, per member).**
   - Potato: tender, knife without resistance [Q234, Q232].
   - Rutabaga: "some give", al dente [Q231] against "no resistance" or "completely tender" [Q232, Q233, Q234]. This is a conflict; if it is still firm, 5 min more under foil [Q231].
   - Cream: no source states a thickened-cream endpoint for a rutabaga gratin. Flour [Q231, G7] and starch [Q235] are the stated thickeners. Curdling is not reported for rutabaga gratins.
   - Top: "bubbly and deeply golden brown" [Q231], "rich golden colour" [Q232], "golden and bubbling" [Q233].
   - Foil first so the top and bottom cook evenly [Q234], or so the top does not burn before the inside cooks [Q201].
6. **Rest (documented finishing stage, not a route step).** "A few minutes" [Q231]; 25 min [Q234]; 1 h held at 50 °C [Q232]. The reason given is heat retention only [Q234]. Setting or cutting is not stated for the rutabaga versions.
- **Alternative sequences (kept separate):**
  - (a) Raw slices, long covered bake, about 90 min or more [G7/Q234, G8]. Untested short raw bakes of about 45 min [Q230, G2, G6] do not fit here.
  - (b) Blanch the rutabaga only, then a bake of about 55-60 min [Q231], or 40-50 min [Q233, G1].
  - (c) Simmer the rutabaga about 10 min in the gratin's cream and broth, then about 25-40 min of baking [G5, G9, Q229].
  - (d) Pre-cook both vegetables (swede 5 min, potato 1 min), then 60-80 min in a bain-marie [Q232].
  - Same dish as the potato [Q231, Q232, G7] against rutabaga-only gratins [Q233, G1, G8]. No source cooks the two separately and combines them later.

### gratin_dauphinois/raw_long_bake
1. **Potato: peel, slice.** About 2 mm [M15a]; 2-3 mm [Q225]; 3 mm [Q203]. Rinsed before slicing [M15a, Q228]; not rinsed after slicing [Q203, Q225, Q235]. An overnight soak is one forum poster's practice [Q219] and conflicts with the no-rinse advice.
2. **Layer in a garlic-rubbed, buttered dish; pour hot milk and cream to cover** [M15a, Q228].
3. **Bake: the only cooking step, so it runs to doneness.**
   - Potato: knife "aucune résistance" (no resistance) [Q203], "très tendres" (very tender) [Q228], "percer facilement" (goes in easily) [Q225].
   - Cream: must not split. A raw assembly with milk only gave "grainy watery liquid" [Q219]. Heat-stable whipping cream is used against curdling [Q238]. Unrinsed starch binds it [Q219, Q235].
   - Top: golden [Q201, Q202].
   - Dependency: slow, low heat is the reason for the long time [M15a]. Foil first avoids a burnt top over a raw interior [Q201, Q225].
   - Skipped or short: raw, not creamy [Q202]; raw inside [Q201]. Overdone: mushy and dried out [Q238].
4. **Rest:** 5-25 min (see pair 13). Serve hot [Q228].
- Times: 1 h at 165 °C [Q228]; 1½-2 h at 160 °C [M15a]; 1 h 30-2 h 30 [Q203]; 45 + 20 min at 180 °C [Q225].

### gratin_dauphinois/simmered_then_baked
1. **Potato: peel, slice 3-4 mm, keep dry and unrinsed** [M15b, Q202, Q235].
2. **Simmer in milk and cream.** Readiness: "tender" or "just tender" [M15b, Q227, Q235], or "softened a little" with the cream slightly thickened [Q238, Q225].
   - Dependency: so the bake can be short (15-20 min [M15b], about 20 min [Q227]); the starch released thickens the sauce [Q235, Q238]; it stops browning [Q235].
   - Failures: boil-over [Q227, Q202]; scorching [Q238, Q235].
3. **Transfer to the buttered dish, liquid up to the top layer** [M15b, Q227].
4. **Bake.** Doneness: blade goes in easily [Q227] and the top is golden [Q235, Q202]. Times: 15-20 min [M15b]; about 20 min [Q227]; 45 min [Q225]; 30 + 30 min [Q235]. Q202 still bakes at least 1 h 30 after the simmer, which conflicts with the short-bake sources.
5. **Rest or chill.** Rest 10 min so the cream sets [Q202], 15 min or more [Q235]. Chill overnight to cut cleanly, then reheat [M15b, Q202, Q235].
   - Make-ahead conflict: break after the simmer and bake next day [Q225] against not stopping halfway [Q235].
