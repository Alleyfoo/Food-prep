# Evidence: soaked-pulse pastes (pairs 1-9: soak, grind, rest, fry, steam)

- Date read: 2026-11-25.
- Method: WebSearch to find, WebFetch to read. WebFetch returns a model-written
  summary of the page, not raw text, so wording and small figures may be off;
  every source below was read that way unless noted. Quotes are at most 15 words,
  used only for endpoint wording, and come through that summary.
- Earlier evidence cited, not re-read: R4 `docs/research/r4-transfers/evidence-pea-falafel.md`
  (P1-P22) and R2 `docs/research/r2-construction-patterns/recipes.md` (M2a-c falafel,
  A3a-b medu vada, A18a-c akara/acarajé, A19a-b moi moi).
- Search languages: Finnish, Swedish, German, English, Arabic, Hebrew, Hindi,
  Tamil (romanised), English-language Nigerian and Indian sources.
- Traps:
  - Pea soaking sources in Finnish, Swedish and German are almost all about
    **soup**, where split peas need no soak. That endpoint belongs to the soup
    role and must not be carried over to the falafel role (see pair 1, by role).
  - No Finnish dried-pea packet text (Pirkka, Myllyn Paras, Rainbow, Kotimaista,
    Lidl) was reachable. The only packet text read is Swedish (GoGreen at ICA, Q102).
  - Mawdoo3 has several falafel pages. Q119 is a different page from M2a/M2b, and
    three of its four recipes use **cooked** chickpeas (an alternative sequence).
  - Several pages give two different soak times on the same page (Q109, Q123, Q127).
    Both are recorded.
  - Search-engine answer text is not evidence. Only fetched pages are cited.

### Q107 — https://www.oekotest.de/essen-trinken/falafel-selber-machen-schnelles-rezept_11791_1.html

### Q108 — https://hurbra.se/falafel/
- Swedish, Sweden. Consumer how-to site, 6 Feb 2026. WebFetch summary.
- Pair 2: dried chickpeas, never cooked ones; cooked chickpeas hold too much water. Soak 12-24 h in plenty of cold water. They should "swell but not cook". Drain and pat dry.
- Pair 6: pulse in short bursts until "grov men formbar" (coarse but formable).
- Pair 7: refrigerate at least 20-30 min so the mix firms and shapes more easily.
- Pair 8:
  - Oil at 170-180 °C. Done when golden brown and crispy outside, soft inside.
  - Failures:
    - Falls apart: cooked or canned chickpeas, or a mix blended too smooth.
    - Greasy and soft: oil too cool.
    - Large balls cook unevenly and hold together less well.
  - Baking powder and 1-2 tbsp flour are added last.
- Alternative sequence: baked at about 225 °C, less crispy.

### Q110 — https://www.nzherald.co.nz/eatwell/food-news/ask-peter-falafels-recipe/JDAWPSX7IYE4AOK622HXX7SS2I

### Q111 — https://cardamomandtea.com/551/the-best-way-to-soak-falafel/
- English, USA. Personal blog with a home soak comparison (Kathryn Pauline), 9 Aug 2020. WebFetch summary.
- Pair 2:
  - Compared 12 h and 16 h at room temperature (about 22 °C), 24 h in the fridge, and 24 h at room temperature with the liquid changed halfway.
  - Recipe: soak at least 16 h, ideally 24 h, at room temperature, loosely covered, with baking soda in plenty of water. Change the soak liquid halfway. Shorten the soak if the kitchen is very hot.
  - Completion:
    - Soft enough to chop easily with a knife, yet still firm and mealy.
    - "hydrated all the way through".
    - Slight sprouting is "the absolute best way to know" they are fully hydrated.
  - Failure: still "hard and dry on the inside" means they need longer.
    - 12 h gave less fluffy falafel.
    - The 24 h fridge soak "did not take in enough water"; refrigeration slows soaking.
  - Shifts: older beans soak much slower; larger chickpeas need the full 24 h; salt in the soak made them slightly less fluffy.
  - Keeps: answering a commenter, the author says a 48 h fridge soak should be fine after a few hours at room temperature first.
- Pair 6: finely minced, no large pieces, not smooth, "resemble very fine couscous". Holds when pressed.
- Pair 8:
  - Oil at 360 °F, kept between 350 and 370 °F. Batches of 5-6, about 4 min, until deep brown but not burnt outside and fluffy inside. Balls at most 1 heaping tbsp.
  - Post-assembly: without baking soda the balls would stay "quite mealy and raw-tasting"; the soda helps them cook through in the fry.

### Q113 — https://plantbasedfolk.com/falafel-falling-apart/
- English. Personal food blog (Janelle Hama), 30 Nov 2023, modified 7 Aug 2024. WebFetch summary.
- Pair 2: overnight soak; drain, and pat dry if needed.
- Pair 6: the target is a homogeneous mix "similar to a grain of couscous". Too coarse or too fine both cause problems.
- Pair 7: chill shaped mix at least 30 min, so it firms and the baking soda has time to work.
- Pair 8, failure "falls apart", with the causes the page gives:
  - Unevenly processed or poorly soaked ingredients.
  - Too wet (the mix "can be easily moved around").
  - Too dry (it crumbles).
  - Wrong grind.
  - Not chilled.
  - Too much baking soda (over-expands).
  - Loosely compacted balls.
  - Reused oil, or too little oil.
  - Oil too hot (outside cooks too fast, inside undercooked).
  - Oil too cool (absorbs oil, mushy).
  - Overcrowding (temperature drops).
  - Rough flipping.
  - Patties too large.
- Pair 8, conditions: target about 180 °C; test with a small piece that should bubble. Turn gently and fry each side golden brown.
- Freezing shaped balls and frying from frozen is offered as a way to prevent breakage.

### Q122 — https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/garelu-medhu-vadai-recipe
- English, India. Personal food blog (Swasthi Shreekanth), first published Jul 2014, updated 4 Jun 2022. WebFetch summary.
- Pair 3:
  - Wash and rub the dal, then soak in fresh water at least 4-5 h (5 h fluffiest). 3 h is possible but gives a different texture. Organic dal up to 8 h.
  - Over-soaked dal holds excess moisture and the vada absorb a lot of oil. No exact cutoff is given.
  - Under-soaked dal gives hard, dense vada.
  - Shifts: old (yellowish) dal gives flat or dense vada; grinder type (readers: 3-4 h ideal for a wet grinder).
- Next stage (grind and aerate):
  - Ice-cold water in small amounts; too much makes a runny batter. Short bursts so the jar never gets warm: heat makes it bitter and hard.
  - **Float test**: a small ball dropped in water should float. If it sinks, the batter is too runny or under-aerated, so beat again.
  - Salt goes into the grind; adding it later thins the batter. This conflicts with A3a and Q124.
- Keeps: batter best the same day, about 2 days in the fridge (readers: 2-3 days wet-grinder batter, 1 day mixer batter). Bring it to near room temperature and beat again.
- Fry, related paste:
  - Medium heat. A test drop should rise without browning much.
  - Too-hot oil browns the outside and leaves the inside raw. Too-cool oil makes the vada absorb oil.
  - Finish about 2 min on a higher flame for colour.

### Q123 — https://www.chitrasfoodbook.com/2014/09/medhu-vadai-recipehow-to-make-urad-dal.html
- English, India (Tamil Nadu). Personal food blog (Chitra Sendhil), 2 Sep 2014, updated 14 Jul 2025. WebFetch summary.
