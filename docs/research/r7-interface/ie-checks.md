# Running the interface checks IE1-IE7

The checks come from R6's register (`../r6-academic-foundations/open-questions.md`).
Each is done by the user, alongside cooking that is due anyway. None takes
more than one evening. Write down what happened in a sentence or two. A
cooking goes into the app as a control record, the observations about the
interface go into this file's log below or into the register.

R7 succeeds if a dinner gets decided and cooked from it with less looking up
than before. A better-looking graph does not count.

## Start here: IE2 with the gratin (KT1)

1. Run the app (`PYTHONPATH=src py -3.11 -m streamlit run app.py`), open
   **Dinner › Dish**. The gratin is the default dish.
2. Before shopping: read the overview once. Is it clear which member is
   prepared on its own (potato, rutabaga) and where they join?
3. While cooking, keep the overview open. For each step note:
   - where you had to stop and work out what to do;
   - any step you mixed up between the two branches (Y703's error type);
   - whether "ready for the next step" against "ready to eat" helped at the
     blanch;
   - whether the per-member lines after the bake told you when to take it
     out (the rutabaga's "some give · or no resistance" is a real
     disagreement between sources).
4. After eating: open **Record what you cooked**. Choose KT1, enter the
   blanch and bake times and where the rutabaga and the potato ended up, and
   give a verdict and notes. Check that the "Your record" lines appear beside
   the documented times and that nothing documented changed.
5. If the oven allows KT8 beside it: record that tray separately with
   **Route followed** set to "raw slices and a long covered bake".

KT3 (napa rolls) is the second candidate for IE2. Use the same steps from the
napa overview.

## IE4: aligned columns against one route with notes

For the gratin, open **Routes side by side**. Choose tonight's way to cook
it from the columns, and say why in one sentence. Then switch back to
**Overview** (one route, with its alternatives as cited notes under "Other
documented ways" at its foot) and say whether the choice would have been different or slower.

## IE1: outcome list against mindmap

On two evenings, decide what to cook from an ingredient you have:
- once with **Dinner › What can I make**;
- once with **Explore › Map**, Cook paths view, the old mindmap.

Note what was cooked, how long deciding took, and what each view made you
look up elsewhere.

## IE3: three absences

A five-minute read-back. Show three cases and ask what each means:
- **K6, not documented:** Dinner › What can I make › kale. There is no
  familiar use, and the only less usual use is a refused swap.
- **K8, documented to fail:** Dinner › Dish › Potato and rutabaga gratin,
  rutabaga raw (control). Its column in the gratin's **Routes side by
  side** is headed "documented to fail".
- **K1-x, not known:** in the gratin's columns, the note under the table
  says that a step from one column with a step from another is a combination
  nobody documented. Ask: "Can I blanch 10 minutes and then bake long?"

The check passes if the three are told apart without explanation.

## IE5: start from a real leftover

The next time there is cooked rice, soaked peas or blanched rutabaga left
over:
1. Open **Dinner › Start from what I have** and choose it.
2. Read how long it keeps and on whose authority.
3. Decide from the routes shown.

Note whether it felt useful or like homework.
