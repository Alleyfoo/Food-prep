# R2 step 3: construction patterns

**Grouping rule.** A preparation goes with others that hold together by the
**same mechanism**: the one whose removal makes the dish fail. Appearance
does not count. The mechanisms (H1-H11) and water routes (W1-W9) are defined
in `mechanisms.md`. Names were given after grouping. Each pattern lists every
recipe behind it, so it can be checked or regrouped.

Nine patterns came out. One finding shapes all the rest: **patterns nest**.
The wrapper of one dish is a mass from another pattern (cepelinai dough is
C2b; arancini rice is C2a), and a coating (C9) can go on any formed mass. So
the patterns are kinds of **structural relationship between components**, not
kinds of dish.

## C1. Released-starch cake

- **Mechanism:** H1. Starch from cut raw tuber cells, kept on the strands or
  recovered from the expressed liquid, gelatinises in hot fat. H9: the crust
  holds the cake until it can be turned.
- **Recipes:**
  - raw rösti (E1b), rårakor (E7a-b), draniki/deruny classic (E5b-c),
    latkes (E4a-b), placki (E3a), Reibekuchen (E2b), gamja-jeon (A10a-b);
  - corpus: about 50 latke recipes return the settled starch.
- **Variants:** egg and flour added on top (most latkes, E2a, E3b, E5a;
  RecipeNLG 91% egg). Bought starch for recovered starch (A10b, E3b).
- **Water:** W2 or W1, W4, W7 (grate just before frying, salt late).
- **Not to be confused with:** C4 (courgette fritters) and C5 (raggmunk),
  which look the same on the plate.

## C2. Cooked-starch mass

- **Mechanism:** H2. A starch that is already gelatinised is cohesive by
  itself once cooled. It is shaped, filled, coated or set in a dish.
- **C2a, cooked and cooled:**
  - boiled rösti (E1a, E1c, E1d Berner), aloo tikki (A2), korokke (A6),
    pastéis de bacalhau (M14), arancini and supplì (M11), croquetas (M13,
    roux-milk gel);
  - pierogi ruskie filling (E16a), vareniki filling (E16b),
    karjalanpiirakka porridge (E15), porkkanalaatikko without egg (E17a);
  - Semmelknödel (E11, soaked bread; egg added).
- **C2b, raw starch bound by cooked or scalded starch (H3):**
  Thüringer Klöße (E10), cepelinai (E9), kroppkakor (E8), pão de queijo
  (A16).
- **Water:** W5. Cook in the skin, drain at once, store for days. Then cool:
  convergence 9 in `mechanisms.md`.
- **Variants:** egg added (M14, E11, A16); cornflour added (A2); none
  (E1a-c, M11, E16a).

## C3. Raw pulse paste

- **Mechanism:** H4. Soaked, uncooked pulses ground with little water; the
  paste sets in hot oil or steam. Often aerated (H11).
- **Recipes:**
  - ta'ameya (M1a-c), Levantine and Sudanese falafel (M2a-c);
  - medu vada (A3a-b), akara (A18a-b), acarajé (A18c, by diffusion);
  - moi moi (A19a-b), steamed in a mould, unaerated, with oil.
- **Water:** the paste is made, not dried. Minimal grinding water and short
  soaks are the control.
- **Not to be confused with:** the cooked-chickpea "falafel" of the American
  corpus (38% canned, 43% flour, 30% crumbs, 28% egg). That is a cooked
  pulse mash held by added binders, closer to C4.

## C4. Bound watery vegetable

- **Mechanism:** H6a + H5b. A watery, starch-poor vegetable, its water
  removed or used, held by a small dry phase (flour, crumbs, cheese, soaked
  bread) and usually egg. Both set by heat.
- **C4a, water removed, then dry phase and egg added:**
  - courgette fritters: mücver (M3), kolokythokeftedes (M4), beignets
    (M18a), Zucchinipuffer (E19a), placki z cukinii (E19b), bolinho (A17a-b);
  - carrot and courgette patties (E18a-d), polpette di melanzane (M10, from
    cooked pulp);
  - spanakopita filling (M8a);
  - corpus: courgette fritters 92% egg, 74% flour; 34% write down removing
    water.
- **C4b, the vegetable's own salt-drawn juice hydrates the flour, no egg
  (H6a'):** onion pakoda (A1a), shredded hobak-jeon (A9b), and bhajiya with
  a splash of water (A1b).
- **Water:** W1, W2 or W6 in C4a; W8 in C4b. Same flour-and-water mechanism,
  opposite water routes.
- **Variants:**
  - finished in the oven with a drier mass (M4a crumb coat; E18b one egg);
  - only the watery vegetable is dewatered in a mix (E18c);
  - raw grated potato instead of crumbs (E18d), a C1 element inside C4.

## C5. Batter matrix with inclusions

- **Mechanism:** H6b. A liquid batter (flour + milk, water or dashi, often
  egg) is the continuous phase; the vegetable is dispersed in it.
- **Recipes:**
  - raggmunk (E6a-b), Corsican beignets de courgettes (M18b);
  - okonomiyaki (A7a-c, with grated yam), kakiage (A5a);
  - corpus: corn fritters (92% flour, 2% water removal).
- **Water:** the vegetable is not dewatered (E6). Wet vegetables go in last
  (A7a), and batter or yam water counts as part of the liquid (A7c).
- **Not to be confused with:** C4. Beignets de courgettes is C4 in M18a and C5
  in M18b: same name, inverted proportions.

## C6. Egg-set matrix

- **Mechanism:** H5a. Egg (or egg + dairy or dashi) is the continuous phase
  and coagulates around separately cooked pieces.
- **Recipes:**
  - tortilla de patatas (M12a-c), frittata (M20a), chawanmushi (A8a);
  - egg-milk porkkanalaatikko (E17b-c), kaalilaatikko with egg-milk (E14a);
  - corpus: quiche (98% egg), frittata (99%), vegetable casseroles set with
    egg (61%).
- **Water:** W5. The pieces are cooked first (M12c: to dehydrate them;
  RecipeNLG 45% of frittatas pre-cook a filling). Cool before the egg goes in
  (E17c, E10c).
- **Variants:** egg can be replaced by starch in the same Finnish bakes (E17a
  porridge only; E14b grain and cream). The egg is a choice there, not the
  structure.

## C7. Container and filling

- **Mechanism:** H7. A shell, leaf or dough holds a filling that need not
  cohere, sometimes with H8 (packing, weight, rest).
- **C7a, edible vessel (a hollowed or opened vegetable):**
  - gemista (M7a-b), farcis niçois (M16a-b), verdure ripiene (M17a-b),
    karnıyarık (M5a-c), chiles rellenos (A14), zha qie he (A12);
  - corpus: stuffed courgette, aubergine, tomato, pepper.
  - The scooped flesh is recombined into the filling (all five
    Mediterranean dishes; RecipeNLG 38% / 27%).
- **C7b, flexible leaf wrap:**
  - cabbage rolls (E12a-b, E13a), yaprak sarma (M6a-b), tamale husk (A15);
  - the inner cabbage leaves go into the filling (E12a).
- **C7c, dough or sheet wrap:**
  - pierogi and vareniki (E16), jiaozi and xianbing (A11), karjalanpiirakka
    (E15), chả giò (A13), filo pies (M8);
  - a C2 mass as the wrapper: kroppkakor and cepelinai (E8, E9), arancini
    (M11a), Thüringer Klöße with bread cubes inside (E10).
- **Water:**
  - The filling is dewatered, cooled or pre-cooked (A11a, E16, M5).
  - Raw rice is the exception: it takes up the liquid inside the container
    (M6, M7a).
  - The wrapper is softened (convergence 10 in `mechanisms.md`).

## C8. Layered assembly

- **Mechanism:** H10. Separately processed layers held by a phase that
  thickens or sets around them (potato starch in dairy, reduced sauce, melted
  cheese, egg in the sauce, custard), and by the dish's walls.
- **Recipes:**
  - gratin dauphinois (M15a-b), parmigiana (M9a-b), moussaka (corpus);
  - kaalilaatikko with reduced cream (E14b);
  - spanakopita and börek (M8a-b), where pastry sheets are the layers.
- **Water:** W1 (salt-drain aubergine), W3 (drain mozzarella), W8 (less
  liquid between yufka sheets, M8b). Chill before cutting (M15b).

## C9. Coated piece

- **Mechanism:** a surface construction: a dry layer, then a wet layer
  (batter, egg, egg foam), sometimes a dry crumb, set by frying (H5c, H5d,
  H9). It is applied to a single raw piece or to a formed mass from another
  pattern.
- **Recipes:**
  - tempura (A4a-b), sliced hobak-jeon (A9a), chiles rellenos capeados
    (A14), zha qie he (A12);
  - korokke (A6a), arancini and supplì (M11), croquetas (M13);
  - corpus: breaded vegetables (63% dredge or coat), aubergine parmigiana
    (61%).
- **Water:** W9. Pat dry; a dry interface layer "absorbs moisture" (A14a).
  The inside must not steam through the coat (A6a); a second fry if it does
  (A12b, A13).

## Cross-cutting principles (true across patterns, not patterns themselves)

1. **Water budget.** Every pattern balances free water. The way it does so
   (W1-W9) is a choice within the pattern, and the same dish allows several
   (M8a vs M8b; A17a vs A17b).
2. **Cool or rest before the next step.** It firms cooked starch (C2), lets a
   dry phase hydrate (C4, C5, E11b), keeps egg from setting on mixing (C6),
   keeps a filling from bursting its coat (C9), and makes a dish sliceable
   (C8).
3. **The final heat is part of the structure.** Crust before turning; hot fat;
   no lid for crusts, a lid for egg tops; starch in dumpling water; simmer,
   not boil, for potato dumplings. Changing the final step changes the
   formulation (convergence 8 in `mechanisms.md`).
4. **Air** (H11) is added inside C3, C5, C6 and masa doughs, and protected by
   folding the rest in last.
