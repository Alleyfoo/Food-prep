# R2 step 1: collected recipes as operation sequences

Recipes where separately processed materials are combined and must hold
together, or stay separate, in a structure. Each was read in the tradition's
own language where possible: 20 languages besides English (English only for
Nigerian and Ethiopian dishes and as second witnesses). Collected on 2026-11-24 by three
research agents, one per region. Their briefs told them not to sort recipes
into roles, only to record what each recipe does, what it says about why, and
where recipes of the same dish disagree.

**How to read this file**
- Steps are **paraphrased**, never copied; quantities appear only where they
  bear on a mechanism. Short phrases in the original language are quoted
  where a page states a reason.
- **Source says** / **[source]**: the page itself states it. **Inference** /
  **[inference]**: the collector's reading, not stated by the page.
- Recipe ids carry a region prefix: **E** (Northern, Central, Eastern Europe
  and Ashkenazi), **M** (Mediterranean, Middle East, Iberia, France),
  **A** (South and East Asia, Latin America, Africa). Inside a region's
  section a bare id such as "7a" means that region's recipe (E7a in the E
  section). The other documents always use the prefix.
- Every URL is in `sources.md`, under the same id. Pages that failed to load
  are listed there and not used.
- Pages were read through a fetch tool that renders and summarises a page, so
  short quotes are as that tool returned them.
- Each region ends with the collector's own observations. The synthesis
  across regions is in `mechanisms.md`.

Corpus evidence (RecipeNLG, mostly North American) is separate:
`recipenlg.md` and `recipenlg-counts.md`.

# E: Northern, Central and Eastern Europe, Ashkenazi

## E1. Rösti (Swiss; German)

### E1a. Caminada, boiled-potato version (gaultmillau.ch, German)
1. Waxy potatoes: boil in skin in salted water until soft, drain, let steam dry briefly.
2. Refrigerate until the next day.
3. Peel cold, grate coarse, salt.
4. Clarified butter in a non-stick pan. Add the potato and press flat.
5. Medium heat about 10 min until golden, turn, another 10 min.
- **Moisture:** pre-cooking, steaming dry and overnight chilling. No squeezing.
- **Holding:** nothing added (no egg, no flour).
- **Reasons:** the source gives none apart from draining fat on paper. The video tagline is "Lassen Sie sich Zeit!"
- **Thermal step:** pan, both sides.

### E1b. Caminada, raw-potato version (same page)
1. Same waxy potatoes, peeled raw.
2. Cut into very fine strips on a vegetable slicer ("Kartoffelspaghetti"), not grated.
3. Lay flat in clarified butter in a non-stick pan, about 6 min per side. Salt after cooking.
- **Moisture:** none mentioned. No squeezing and no rinsing.
- **Holding:** nothing added.
- **Note:** the raw version cooks in roughly half the time and is thinner. Lecker (search snippet only, page not read) says raw Rösti must be thin so it cooks through.

### E1c. Claudio Del Principe in Blick (blick.ch, Swiss German press)
1. Boil potatoes in skin. Leave about 1 kg unpeeled to dry for **two days**. He cites chefs who instead chill the peeled potato 24 h, grate it, then chill another 24 h.
2. Peel and grate into long strands on a Röstiraffel.
3. Enough clarified butter to cover the pan bottom (non-stick or seasoned cast iron).
4. Add the strands in portions and toss them gently until the surfaces are sealed. Salt only lightly at this stage.
5. Once the pan is full, push everything into one cake and stop disturbing it.
6. Add more fat, swirl, turn with a lid or plate, fry the second side. Moderate heat, "nicht hetzen".
7. Finish on high heat with fresh butter taken to beurre noisette, about 1 min per side. Coarse sea salt.
- **Moisture:** pre-cooking plus multi-day storage. **Source says** this is "entscheidend" for a crisp Rösti, and that the potato keeps losing moisture and collapses in the pan.
- **Holding:** nothing added. The cake forms from strands that have already been sealed in fat, then pressed together.
- **Surprising:** the strands are sealed in fat *before* the cake is formed. That is two thermal phases (loose, then compacted).

### E1d. Encyclopedic witness (austria-forum.org AustriaWiki "Rösti", German; a wiki mirror, not a recipe)
- Berner Rösti is "stets aus gekochten" potatoes, with bacon and onion, and is splashed with milk after the first browning.
- **Zürcher Rösti is made from raw potato that is grated, rinsed and dried**, then fried in clarified butter.
- Floury potatoes are called less suitable. When served with a saucy dish the Rösti "muss ... saugfähig sein", so pre-cooked potato is preferred there.
- **Contradiction:** washing the raw grated potato (Zürich, per this witness) is the opposite of the "don't rinse, the starch glues" advice in rårakor (see 7a). A search-result summary also claims "only the starch holds Rösti together" (page not read, so not cited). Recipes 1a–1c add nothing and hold anyway.

## E2. Kartoffelpuffer / Reibekuchen (German)

### E2a. Wikibooks Kochbuch "Kartoffelpuffer" (de.wikibooks.org, German)
1. Grate 1 kg raw peeled potato and 1 large onion.
2. Mix in 3 eggs, 4 tbsp wheat flour, salt, pepper, nutmeg.
3. Fry oval flat cakes in oil, crisp on both sides.
- **Moisture:** none stated for Puffer. The same page says Rösti potato should be "gut ausgedrückt", and that the starch that collects can be used.
- **Holding:** egg + flour (about 3 eggs / 4 tbsp flour per kg). No reason given.
- **Same page:** a regional *Topfkuchen* (Dibbelabbes and similar names) bakes the same batter in a covered pot in the oven for about 1 h, then 1 h uncovered. That is the same mass with a different thermal step.

### E2b. Rievkooche, Cologne (koeln.de, German)
1. Grate 1 kg peeled potato *into a sieve* over a bowl. Let stand about 15 min.
2. Pour off the water and keep the settled starch ("abgesetzten Stärkemehl"). Return the potato mass to the starch.
3. Add 2 eggs, salt, 2 tbsp flour **or oat flakes**, 1 grated onion, and optionally parsley or leek.
4. Form small cakes and fry crisp in hot fat. Drain on paper.
- **Moisture:** gravity draining through the sieve, no squeezing. The liquid's starch goes back in.
- **Holding:** settled potato starch + egg + flour/oats.
- **Reasons:** none given.

## E3. Placki ziemniaczane (Polish)

### E3a. "wg Magdy Gessler" (smaker.pl user recipe, Polish)
1. Grate 1 kg potatoes and 2 onions on a **fine** grater.
2. Tip onto a sieve over a bowl and press gently.
3. Mix the potato with 2 eggs, 2–3 tbsp wheat flour per kg, optional garlic.
4. Pour the juice off the bowl gently, leaving the potato starch at the bottom. Add that starch to the mass with 1 tbsp sour cream. **Source says** the sour cream "zapobiegnie jej ciemnieniu" (prevents darkening).
5. Season and fry in oil or lard, golden on both sides.
- *Spot check 2026-11-24 (raw page text): step order corrected; egg and flour go in before the starch is returned.*
- **Holding:** returned starch + egg + flour.

### E3b. "Chrupiące placki ziemniaczane" (smaker.pl user recipe, Polish)
1. Grate 700 g peeled potato with 2 onions on a fine grater.
2. Add 2 eggs, 3 heaped tbsp wheat flour **and** 1 heaped tbsp potato starch.
3. Spoon thin cakes into well-heated oil, golden on both sides. Serve at once.
- **Moisture:** **no draining or squeezing at all.** The liquid stays in, and the dry additions (flour + bought starch) take it up. *Inference.*
- **Contrast with 3a:** 3a recovers the potato's own starch; 3b buys starch and keeps the water. Both use egg + flour.

## E4. Latkes / levivot (Ashkenazi, Hebrew sources)

### E4a. kipa.co.il (Hebrew), "the trick so my latkes never fall apart"
1. Grate 4 medium potatoes and 1 small onion coarse into a bowl.
2. Let stand about **1 minute**. Pour off the liquid on top and keep the starch at the bottom.
3. Squeeze gently. Add 1 egg and 1 tbsp flour or potato starch, salt, pepper.
4. Wide pan, oil about 0.5 cm deep, medium-high heat. Form flat cakes with a spoon.
5. 2–3 min per side. Turn only when the cake releases from the pan.
- **Source says:** the starch "thickens and sets on heating" (העמילן מסמיך ומתייצב בעת החימום), which keeps the latkes stable.

### E4b. Pascal Perez-Rubin in Maariv (maariv.co.il, Hebrew, 2017)
1. Grate 1 kg peeled potatoes coarse. **Waxy** varieties are recommended; **source says** they absorb less oil.
2. Strain through a mesh sieve into a bowl and let rest about 15 min.
3. Pour off the liquid and keep the starchy sediment. **Source says** it helps the latkes bind.
4. Add 3 eggs, 3 tbsp sifted flour, a pinch of sugar, salt.
5. Heat oil in a wide pan (wooden-spoon bubble test). Flatten spoonfuls and fry until the edges colour and lift, then turn.
6. Never add cold oil mid-batch. **Source says** the latkes soak up cold oil.
- **Also says:** raw grated gives a crisp latke, while a boiled-and-mashed version gives a smooth one.
- **Agreement and difference:** 4a and 4b both keep the starch and both use egg + flour. They differ on standing time (1 min vs 15 min) and on egg level (about 1 egg per 4 potatoes vs 3 eggs per kg).

## E5. Draniki / deruny (Belarusian, Russian, Ukrainian)

### E5a. Tinkoff Journal (t-j.ru, Russian)
1. Grate potato and onion coarse.
2. **Salt, cover and rest 5–7 min.**
3. Press through a colander or cheesecloth and keep the juice. **Source says** "Важно хорошо отжать жидкость", so they cook evenly and don't fall apart.
4. Mix the potato with 1 egg, 1 tbsp flour per 600 g potato and salt. The flour is kept low on purpose so the potato taste dominates.
5. When a sediment has formed in the juice, pour off the liquid and stir the starch into the potato. No reason is given for this step.
6. Pan with oil, about 1 tbsp of mass each, pressed lightly. Medium heat, 4–5 min per side. Don't turn before the golden crust forms.
- *Spot check 2026-11-24 (raw page text): step order corrected; an earlier "about 10 min" standing time and a stated reason for returning the starch ("densifies the mass") are not on the page and were removed.*
- **Reader comments (not the author's method; not visible in the raw re-read, so unverified):** Belarusian commenters describe potato + salt only, and say egg and flour turn draniki into "оладьи" (pancakes). They prefer the finest grater.

### E5b. AiF (aif.ru, Russian, 2014), presented as the Belarusian classic
1. Have everything ready before you start. Grate the potato **last**, on the **finest** grater, together with onion. **Source says** "Луковый сок не даст картошке потемнеть".
2. Squeeze quickly through cheesecloth, then immediately add a little milk or sour cream.
3. Let the juice settle and return the starch. **Source says** that with the starch, flour is not needed.
4. The classic Belarusian version has **no egg or flour**. This recipe adds 1 egg + 2 tbsp flour to about 5 potatoes (optionally 2 whites instead of the whole egg), plus a pinch of soda.
5. Fry in lard (preferred), neutral oil or clarified butter, not plain butter, in a very well-heated pan. Medium heat, both sides.

### E5c. TSN (tsn.ua, Ukrainian), "deruny without flour or eggs, grandmothers' method"
1. Use starchy potatoes, grated fine.
2. Put in a sieve or cloth and press out **some** liquid gently.
3. Let the juice stand 5–10 min, pour off the liquid and return the starch. **Source says** the starch helps deruny "stay whole when frying".
4. Grate in a little onion. **Source says** it slows darkening.
5. **Salt just before frying.** **Source says** salt draws liquid, so salting early makes the mass runnier.
6. Well-heated pan, thin layer of oil, thin cakes with gaps between them. Medium to medium-high heat. **No lid**; **source says** trapped steam softens the crust.
7. If the mass is too loose, drain again and return the starch. The source advises against flour.
- **Contradiction:** 5a salts early on purpose, then squeezes, so salt works as a dewatering step. 5c salts late so the mass doesn't release water. Both use the same mechanism (salt draws water) in opposite ways.

## E6. Raggmunk (Swedish)

### E6a. Filip Fasten (koket.se, Swedish)
1. Whisk flour, salt, milk and egg into a smooth batter (2 dl flour, 4 dl milk, 1 egg per 800 g firm potato).
2. Peel the potato and grate it coarse **directly into the batter**.
3. Fry about 1.5 dl portions in butter until golden brown.
- **Moisture:** **not squeezed.** The potato goes into a wet milk batter.
- **Holding:** a pancake batter (flour + egg + milk) that sets around the potato strands.
- **Reasons:** none given.

### E6b. Visit Sweden (visitsweden.com, English, second witness)
1. Make a batter of milk + flour, and egg with salt (about 1 egg and ¾ cup flour per 800 g potato).
2. Grate the potato fine or coarse; the source says it is a matter of taste. Mix into the batter. **Rest 30 min.**
3. Butter in a pan, about ½ cup per pancake. Spread so the potato is evenly distributed, about American-pancake thickness.
4. About 4 min per side at medium heat. **Source says** too hot and the pancakes colour "before the potatoes are actually cooked".
- **Agreement and difference:** both use no squeezing and a milk-flour-egg batter. 6b rests 30 min; 6a grates straight into the batter and fries.

## E7. Rårakor (Swedish, Finland-Swedish)

### E7a. hurbra.se (Swedish)
1. Use firm potato (e.g. Amandine). Grate on the **coarse** side **just before frying**.
2. Squeeze in a clean towel until damp, not wet. **Do not rinse.** **Source says** the starch "hjälper strimlorna att fästa vid varandra".
3. Basic version: no egg, no flour. Some recipes add them.
4. Salt can go on after frying. **Source says** salt makes potato release liquid.
5. Oil + butter. The pan must be hot when the potato goes in; **source says** a cold pan makes it absorb fat and steam.
6. About 100 g potato (weighed before squeezing) per cake, flattened thin and even.
7. 3–5 min undisturbed, then turn in one motion. **Source says** don't turn too early, because once the surface has set the strands hold together much better.
- **Why they break (source):** too big or thick, not enough crust before turning, or too wet.

### E7b. Martha (martha.fi, Finland-Swedish, "Rårakor med norsrom")
1. Use firm potatoes, grated **fine**.
2. Squeeze out the liquid thoroughly ("krama ur vätskan väl"). Season with salt and pepper.
3. Slightly above medium heat, butter + rapeseed oil. Thin cakes about 15 cm across.
- **Holding:** nothing added.
- **Agreement and difference:** 7a and 7b agree on squeezing with no binder. They disagree on grate size (coarse vs fine) and on salt timing (after vs before frying).

## E8. Kroppkakor (Swedish, filled potato dumpling)

### E8a. "Mormor Janes kroppkakor" (koket.se, Swedish)
1. Grate 1 kg raw firm potato. Squeeze in a cloth, "så mycket vätska som möjligt".
2. Mash 300 g boiled potato and add it to the raw mass.
3. Add 2 dl wheat flour and a little salt, and work into a smooth dough.
4. Filling: diced salt pork belly, onion, allspice (raw).
5. Roll into a log and cut thick pieces. Make a hollow in each, fill, close by hand and roll gently round.
6. Lower into boiling salted water and simmer about 45 min. Done when they float.
- **Moisture:** hard squeezing. The liquid and its starch are **discarded** (not mentioned as returned).
- **Holding:** boiled potato + wheat flour.
- **Note:** a search snippet (Wikipedia, not read in full) says the raw:boiled ratio varies regionally from almost all raw (Blekinge "grey") to all boiled (Gotland). Treat as unverified.

## E9. Cepelinai (Lithuanian)

### E9a. Maxima food experts (Brigita Baratinskaitė) in alkas.lt (Lithuanian)
1. Prefer late-harvest potatoes; **source says** they have more starch.
2. Boil 5 potatoes in skin, peel, mash. The ratio is about 1 part boiled to 3 parts raw. **Source says** "Virtos bulvės leis masei lengviau sukibti ir neištižti verdant".
3. Grate 5 raw potatoes fine and drain by spoon or hand pressure.
4. Combine, add 200 g starch (type unspecified) and salt, knead.
5. Filling: pork neck mince, grated onion, salt, pepper.
6. Form around the filling.
7. **One glass of the potato liquid (after the starch settles) goes into the cooking water.** **Source says** this helps the dumplings keep shape.
8. Add the dumplings only when the water is at a full boil. **Source says** otherwise they "iškart ištižti" (turn mushy immediately). Cook about 30 min.

### E9b. TV3 (tv3.lt, Lithuanian)
1. Peel and grate 1 kg raw potato and squeeze it out.
2. Mash 300 g boiled potato and mix in. Salt.
3. Filling: ground beef-pork with onions fried in fat.
4. Egg-sized pieces, flattened thin, filled, sealed, shaped oval.
5. Boil in salted boiling water 15–20 min.
- **No added starch or flour.**
- **Agreement and difference:** both combine raw and boiled potato. 9a adds bought starch and puts settled-liquid starch into the *cooking water*. 9b adds nothing.

## E10. Thüringer Klöße / rohe and halb-und-halb Kartoffelklöße (German)

### E10a. Küchengötter "Thüringer Klöße" (kuechengoetter.de PDF, German)
1. Peel and finely grate 700 g potatoes boiled in their skins the day before.
2. Fry small bread cubes in butter until crisp.
3. Peel and finely grate 2 kg floury raw potatoes. Press in portions through a damp Kloßsack or towel. Let the pressed water stand until the starch settles.
4. **Scald the dry raw mass with about a cup of boiling water**, then add the settled starch (without water) and the cooked potato. Salt.
5. Shape with wet hands, push 3–4 bread cubes into the centre and close.
6. Into simmering salted water, low heat, about 30 min, "ziehen (nicht kochen!)".

### E10b. t-online (t-online.de, German)
1. Use 3 kg floury potatoes. Cook 1 kg diced until very soft and keep 2 kg raw.
2. Grate the raw potato and squeeze in small batches through a towel, catching the liquid. Fluff the mass with a fork.
3. Let the starch settle, pour off the water, return the starch.
4. Press the cooked potato through a sieve into the raw mass (it is still hot, *inference from sequence*). Salt.
5. Wet hands, 12 dumplings, toasted bread cubes inside.
6. Into boiling salted water, then reduce heat, about 20 min.
- **Reasons:** none stated on the page.

### E10c. Öko-Test overview (oekotest.de, German)
- **Thüringer:** about 2:1 raw:cooked. Squeeze the raw potato hard and press the cooked potato **while hot**. Hot mash is poured over the raw mass, the settled starch is returned, and there are bread cubes inside.
- **Halb und halb:** 1:1, settled starch plus 2–3 tbsp bought potato starch, egg optional.
- **Seidenknödel:** cooked potato only, about 120 g potato starch + 1 egg. **Source says** let the mash cool somewhat "damit das Ei beim Vermengen nicht stockt", and over-kneading makes them tough.
- **Floury potatoes:** **source says** they have "mehr Stärke und weniger Wasser". Boiled potatoes are cooled overnight to be drier.
- **Test dumpling:** cook one first. If it falls apart, add starch or egg by the spoonful; if too firm, add a little clear cooking water.
- **Source says** raw mass must be worked immediately or it turns grey and releases liquid again.
- **Source says** cook just below the boil; strong boiling bumps them and the mass "reißt sonst auf oder zerfällt".
- **Agreement:** 10a and 10c both pre-heat the raw mass (boiling water, or hot mash). 10b mixes in cooked potato pressed straight into it. All three return the settled starch, and none uses flour or egg in the Thüringer form.
- **Contradiction with 9a:** Klöße go into water held below the boil; cepelinai (9a) must go into a full boil.

## E11. Semmelknödel (German / Austrian)

### E11a. lecker.de (German)
1. Cut 5 stale rolls (about 300 g) into 1 cm cubes.
2. Pour 250 ml warm milk over and stir. Soak about 20 min; harder rolls need longer.
3. Sweat 2 small onions in butter for about 1 min until glassy.
4. Add onion, parsley and 3 beaten eggs, knead by hand, season.
5. Shape 8–10 with wet hands.
6. Into boiling salted water, then low heat for 15–20 min until they rise and float.
- **Holding:** soaked bread + egg. **No flour.**
- **Reasons:** only that wet hands make shaping easier.

### E11b. Seminarbäuerinnen Oberösterreich (ooe.lfi.at PDF, Austrian German)
1. Fry the onion golden in butter and pour it over 250 g Knödelbrot.
2. Add parsley, 380 ml milk and 3 eggs, mix well, season. Let it **"durchziehen" about 15 min**.
3. **Only then work in 2 tbsp flour.** Shape with wet hands.
4. Simmer gently ("sanft köcheln") in plenty of salted water, 12–15 min.
- **Holding:** soaked bread + egg + flour, with the flour added after hydration.
- **Agreement and difference:** both hydrate dry bread before shaping and cook at a gentle simmer. 11b uses more milk per bread (about 1.5 ml/g vs 0.8 ml/g) and compensates with flour. 11a uses no flour.

## E12. Kaalikääryleet / kåldolmar (Finnish, Finland-Swedish)

### E12a. Henri Alén (mtvuutiset.fi, Finnish)
1. Core the cabbage. Boil the whole head about 10 min and peel the leaves off whole.
2. Chop the small inner leaves into the filling.
3. Brown the mince, then add leek, garlic, chopped cabbage heart and **cooked** barley or rice (2 dl per 400 g meat). Fry briefly and season. **No egg.**
4. Spoon onto leaves, fold the sides and roll tight.
5. Place side by side in a dish, pour dark syrup over, optional butter, a splash of water on the bottom.
6. Oven 170 °C about 40 min.
- **Note:** the filling meat is pre-browned, so it holds as loose crumbs inside a leaf wrap. The wrap does the holding. *Inference.*

### E12b. Yle Svenska "kåldolmar" (yle.fi, Finland-Swedish)
1. Cut around the root and boil the head root-side up in salted water. Remove leaves as they soften and drain them.
2. Trim the thick rib. **Source says** it makes the leaves easier to roll.
3. Filling: 3 dl **cooked** rice + 350 g **raw** pork mince + 1.5 dl milk, salt, pepper. **No egg.** The source calls the filling loose, so a heaped tbsp per leaf.
4. Fold the sides and roll into packets.
5. **Either** oven: syrup over, water in the bottom, 200 °C about 1.5 h. **Or** brown in butter seam-side down with syrup, then braise covered about 30 min.
- **Agreement and difference:** both use cooked grain, no egg, syrup and an oven. 12a uses browned meat and no liquid in the filling; 12b uses raw meat + milk, which sets as the meat cooks. *Inference.*

## E13. Gołąbki (Polish)

### E13a. "Babcine gołąbki" (smaker.pl editorial, Polish)
1. Cook short-grain rice **fully** per the packet and cool it.
2. Soften the onion in oil. Mix half of it with raw beef-pork mince, the cooled rice, **1 egg**, salt, pepper.
3. Core the cabbage. Bring the whole head to a boil in water and separate the large leaves. **Cut out** the hard rib.
4. Fill and fold.
5. Line the pot bottom and sides with leftover leaves. Pack the rolls "tak, by się nie rozwinęły". Cover with more leaves.
6. Water to ¾ of the rolls' height. Low heat about 40 min.
7. Pour off the broth and make a sauce (tomato concentrate, cream, onion). Pour it back and simmer a little longer.
- **Holding:** egg in the filling + tight packing in the pot.
- **Compare with 12:** the Finnish rolls use no egg and bake in the oven; the Polish roll adds egg and stews in a packed pot lined with leaves.
- **Note:** a search snippet claimed none of the sources used half-cooked rice. I did not verify other Polish recipes for raw or half-cooked rice.

## E14. Kaalilaatikko (Finnish cabbage bake, set or unset)

### E14a. MTV 1999 (mtvuutiset.fi, Finnish)
1. Shred 1 kg cabbage. Either sauté in fat or simmer in a little water until soft; the source calls simmering the option "heikkovatsaisten" (for sensitive stomachs).
2. Cook 2 dl rice or pearl barley per the packet (can be done the day before).
3. Brown 400 g mince **without added fat**, optional onion.
4. Mix all and put into greased dish(es).
5. Pour over **either** about 4 dl meat broth **or** an egg-milk of 6 dl milk + 3–4 eggs. Season, optional crumbs and butter.
6. 150–180 °C about 1.5 h.
- **Surprising:** the same bake can be finished unset (broth) or set (egg custard). The recipe treats this as a free choice.

### E14b. Rouva Kasvis, Pirjo Toikkanen (mtvuutiset.fi, Finnish)
1. Cut the cabbage into strips and simmer gently in salted water while preparing the rest. **Source says** the secret is not browning it dark and not stewing it long.
2. Cook the rice-barley mix about 20 min and drain.
3. Fry onion, then the mince, season.
4. Mix cabbage (with a little of its liquid), meat, grain, syrup and 2 dl cream.
5. Shallow buttered dish. **Source says** this gives "paljon ... paistopintaa".
6. 175 °C about 1 h. Brush during baking with stock + syrup; **source says** it gives the surface flavour and colour.
- **No egg.** This is a moist bake held by the grain and reduced cream.

## E15. Karjalanpiirakka (Finnish, Karelian)

### E15a. MTV (mtvuutiset.fi, Finnish)
1. Dough: salt in 2 dl water + 4 dl rye flour, kneaded smooth. **No leavening.**
2. Porridge: 2.5 dl water + 20–30 g butter. When hot, add 2 dl porridge rice + 7 dl milk and cook about 45 min. Salt. **Cool under a lid in a cool place.**
3. Roll the dough into a log, cut 1.5 cm pieces and roll each thin into an oval with rye flour.
4. Spread porridge in the middle and pinch the edges up over it.
5. 270 °C about 8 min. Brush with melted butter "heti paiston jälkeen".

### E15b. Seija Pirttikoski "Piirakkamummo" (kaleva.fi, Finnish)
1. Dough: 5 dl water, salt, 1 tbsp oil, 0.5 dl wheat, 13 dl rye (wheat is about 4%).
2. Rope, pieces, balls, flattened, rolled very thin; "Taikinan on oltava ohut", thin enough to see "five churches" through it.
3. Porridge cooked in full-fat milk **the day before**. The article says the filling is porridge **and egg** (method of adding not described).
4. Fill and crimp. **Source says** the porridge must not spill onto the dough edge, so the pie keeps its shape.
5. Electric oven at 300 °C, a tray about every 15 min. Brush hot pies with real melted butter.
- **Agreement:** both use an unleavened rye-water dough rolled very thin, porridge cooked long in milk and cooled or made a day ahead, a very hot short bake, and butter brushed on right after baking.
- **Difference:** 15b may stir egg into the porridge; 15a does not.

## E16. Filled boiled dumplings: pierogi ruskie (Polish) and vareniki with potato (Russian)

### E16a. "Prawdziwe ruskie pierogi babci Irenki" (smaker.pl, Polish)
1. Filling: boil potatoes in salted water, **cool**, press through a ricer. Fry onion golden in butter with a pinch of salt and a little sugar, and cool it. Mash full-fat twaróg + bryndza with a fork. Combine, add white pepper and nutmeg, mix until it holds. **No egg.**
2. Dough: about 200 g flour, 5 tbsp neutral oil + 5 tbsp olive oil, ½ cup hot (not boiling) water, salt. **No egg.** Keep the portions covered somewhere warm. Roll to 1.5–2 mm. **Source says** the oil means even thin dough "nie rozklei się podczas gotowania".
3. Cut with a glass, about ½ tsp filling each, seal with fingers then a fork.
4. Into hot salted water. 2–3 min after they float.
- **Holding (filling):** cooled riced potato + crumbly curd cheese. Dry components bind each other with no added binder. *Inference.* The source gives no reason for cooling.

### E16b. Halva Media "vareniki s kartoshkoi" (media.halvacard.ru, Russian)
1. Filling: boil the potatoes and mash with a little cooking water **or** hot milk, plus butter and salt. Add onion fried golden (optional cracklings). Use the filling cooled ("остывшей").
2. For a drier filling, dice potato and onion, salt and let the juice drain in a colander.
3. Dough: 400 g flour, 160 ml **boiling** water, 1 egg, 2 tbsp oil, salt. **Source says** boiling water makes it elastic. Rest 40 min under a towel.
4. Brush the edges with egg white before sealing.
5. Boil 5–7 min after floating, and lift out promptly. **Source says** overcooking makes them lose shape and turn sticky. Salt in the dough is said to keep them from "развариться или расклеиться".
6. Optional: 1.5 tbsp rice in the cooking water. **Source says** the starchy water stops the dumplings falling apart.
- **Convergence with 9a:** starch is deliberately put into the *cooking water* (rice here, potato-liquid starch in cepelinai) to keep the shells intact. This is the source's claim in both.

## E17. Porkkanalaatikko (Finnish carrot bake)

### E17a. MTV "Lanttu- ja porkkanalaatikko" (mtvuutiset.fi, Finnish)
1. Boil carrots until done and purée in a blender.
2. Season with a little syrup and nutmeg.
3. Mix into 1 l of ready-made rice porridge (method not given) and pour into a dish.
4. Pattern the top with a spoon, add crumbs and butter.
5. 150 °C for **at least 2 h**.
- **Holding:** rice porridge alone. **Egg-free** (tagged "Kananmunaton").

### E17b. Kotiliesi "Porkkanalaatikko" (kotiliesi.fi, Finnish)
1. Boil 1.5 dl porridge rice in 5 dl **water** until the water is absorbed.
2. Whisk 2 eggs into 5 dl milk. Grate 800 g **raw** carrot.
3. Stir carrot + egg-milk into the rice. Season (no syrup).
4. Greased dish, almonds and butter on top.
5. 160–175 °C for 1–1.5 h.
- **Holding:** water-cooked rice + egg-milk custard that sets in the oven. The raw carrot cooks in the dish. *Inference.*
- **Source says** it may be frozen unbaked, but don't overbake one that is reheated.

### E17c. K-ruoka "Perinteinen porkkanalaatikko" (k-ruoka.fi, Finnish)
1. Boil 1 kg chopped carrots in 5 dl water. Pour off most of the liquid, keeping about 1 dl, purée and **cool**.
2. Porridge: 1.5 dl rice in 2 dl water until absorbed, then 5 dl whole milk for about 40 min until thick. **Cool.**
3. Mix purée + porridge, then 2 eggs, 2 tbsp syrup, salt, white pepper, nutmeg.
4. Greased dish, crumbs, spoon pattern.
5. 175 °C about 1 h.
- **Reasons:** none stated for cooling. *Inference:* it keeps the egg from setting on mixing. Öko-Test states exactly this reason for Seidenknödel (10c).
- **Three-way comparison:** the 17a porridge-only bake is egg-free with a long low bake. 17b uses raw grated carrot + water-cooked rice + egg custard. 17c uses mashed carrot + milk porridge + egg. Egg is optional; the rice is common to all three.

## E18. Vegetable patties: morotsbiffar (Swedish, Finland-Swedish), porkkanapihvit (Finnish)

### E18a. Martha "Morots-halloumibiffar" (martha.fi, Finland-Swedish)
1. Grate 250 g carrot fine and 250 g aged/grilling cheese coarse.
2. Mix with 2 eggs and ¾ dl breadcrumbs or panko, chili, pepper; salt only if needed.
3. Shape 8 with oiled hands, turn in crumbs, brush both sides with oil.
4. Oven 250 °C for 15–20 min, or pan-fry at medium heat.
- **Moisture:** none handled. Carrot is not squeezed.
- **Holding:** egg + crumbs + grated cheese.

### E18b. Ingmar "Morots- och halloumibiffar" (ingmar.app, Swedish)
1. Grate 250 g carrot coarse and 200 g halloumi.
2. Mix with **1 egg** + ½ dl crumbs + spices.
3. 9 patties on paper, 250 °C 10 min, brush with oil, turn, 10 min more.
- **Source says:** 1 egg works in the oven but would be harder in a frying pan. 2 eggs hold "betydligt bättre" but taste of egg.
- **Surprising:** the author links the amount of binder to the cooking method. The oven tolerates a weaker binder than the pan.

### E18c. Knatteplock "Zucchini- och morotsbiffar med halloumi" (knatteplock.se, Swedish)
1. Grate zucchini, carrot and halloumi coarse.
2. **Press the liquid out of the zucchini, then leave it on paper with a little salt** "för att minska vätskan ytterligare". The carrot is not treated.
3. Mix with 2 eggs, spices and 1 dl crumbs into a thick batter. Let it **"svälla i 5 minuter"**.
4. Shape about 8 and fry in oil, turning carefully.
- **Note:** only the watery vegetable (zucchini) gets dewatering. The carrot goes in raw and unsqueezed.

### E18d. MTV "Porkkanapihvit" (mtvuutiset.fi, Finnish)
1. Grate raw carrots and potatoes fine. **Not squeezed.**
2. Mix with 1–2 eggs, toasted sunflower seeds, salt, pepper.
3. Shape 12 and brown first in butter in a pan.
4. Finish in a greased dish in the oven (150 °C, about 6 min).
- **Holding:** egg + raw grated potato (its starch, *inference*) + seeds. **No flour or crumbs.**
- **Agreement and difference:** 18a–18c (Swedish) all use egg + breadcrumbs. The Finnish 18d replaces crumbs with raw potato. All four use a two-surface finish (pan, or an oven with oil on both sides).

## E19. Zucchini fritters: Zucchinipuffer (German), placki z cukinii (Polish)

### E19a. lecker.de "Knusprige Zucchinipuffer" (German)
1. Make a batter **first**: 120 g flour + salt + pepper, whisked with 6 tbsp milk, 6 tbsp cream and 2 eggs. Let it **swell about 10 min**.
2. Grate 600 g zucchini fine, put in a clean towel and squeeze hard. Mix into the batter.
   - **Source says** otherwise the dough gets too wet, and (FAQ) the Puffer hold together poorly and don't crisp.
   - Optional: salt the shreds and let stand about 10 min before squeezing to release more water.
3. 4–6 tbsp oil per batch, Puffer lying fully in oil, medium-high heat. **Source says** a too-cold pan makes the vegetable soak up fat.
4. About 1 heaped tbsp each, flattened, 3–4 min per side. **Source says** turn only when the underside is firm, or the Puffer falls apart.
- Variants on the same page: oat flakes instead of flour; vegan with soaked linseed + flour; low-carb with almond flour + parmesan.

### E19b. smaker.pl "Placki z cukinii" (Polish user recipe)
1. Grate 400 g zucchini medium. **Salt and leave 15 min.**
2. Squeeze out excess water.
3. Add 1 large egg, 4 tbsp wheat flour, garlic, dill, salt, pepper.
4. Spoonfuls into hot oil, golden on both sides.
- **Agreement:** both dewater (19a by squeeze with optional salting; 19b by salt, rest, squeeze) and bind with egg + flour.
- **Difference:** 19a builds a liquid batter first and then adds the squeezed vegetable, which is structurally like raggmunk (6). 19b adds the dry binders straight to the vegetable.
- **Cross-dish convergence:** salt-then-squeeze for zucchini (19b, 18c, 19a optional) is the same move as draniki 5a's salt-rest-squeeze for potato.

## Collector's observations (E)

1. **Strong convergence on recovering potato starch from the liquid.** Six traditions independently drain the grated raw potato, let the liquid settle, pour off the water and put the white sediment back:
   - Cologne Reibekuchen (2b)
   - Polish placki (3a)
   - Hebrew latkes (4a, 4b)
   - Russian, Belarusian and Ukrainian draniki and deruny (5a–5c)
   - Thüringer Klöße (10a–10c)
   - Swedish rårakor, by a variant: keep the starch by *not rinsing* (7a)

   Several sources state the same reason in their own words: it binds, or it thickens and sets on heating. Ukrainian 5c and AiF 5b say the returned starch makes flour unnecessary.
2. **Divergence from that convergence.** Some dishes deliberately discard the water or don't dewater at all:
   - **Discard and use nothing:** Rösti uses no binder, in both the raw and the pre-boiled form. The Zürcher variant (1d) even *rinses* the grated potato.
   - **Don't squeeze:** raggmunk (6a, 6b) and one placki recipe (3b) leave the water in. Raggmunk relies on a milk-flour-egg pancake batter as the matrix around the strands, and 3b adds bought starch + flour to absorb the liquid.
   - **Contradictory advice for the same goal (a cohesive potato cake):** "don't rinse, starch glues" (rårakor) and "rinse and dry" (Zürcher Rösti).
3. **Pre-cooking as an alternative to a binder (and as a binder).**
   - Boiled-and-aged Rösti (1a, 1c) holds with no addition. Blick ties multi-day ageing and drying to crispness and to avoiding mush.
   - In dumplings, cooked potato is *the* binder for raw potato. Three regions converge on a raw + cooked mix: Thüringer Klöße (2:1), Lithuanian cepelinai (3:1 to 1kg:300g) and Swedish kroppkakor (1kg:300g + flour). Cepelinai 9a states the reason: boiled potato helps the mass bind and not go mushy.
   - Thüringer 10a/10c go further and **scald the raw mass** with boiling water or hot mash before shaping.
4. **Starch into the cooking water.** Cepelinai (9a: a glass of potato liquid) and vareniki (16b: a spoon of rice) both add starch to the boiling water so the dumplings keep their shape (source claims). This is a convergence I did not expect.
5. **Water temperature for dumplings contradicts.**
   - Thüringer Klöße: "ziehen (nicht kochen!)", just below the boil, or they tear.
   - Semmelknödel: low heat, gentle simmer.
   - Cepelinai (9a): water must be at a full boil when the dumplings go in, or they disintegrate at once.
   - The cepelinai claim concerns the entry moment, the Klöße claim the whole cook. They may not truly conflict, but they are stated as opposites.
6. **Salt timing is used in opposite directions.**
   - **Salt early to pull water out, then squeeze:** draniki 5a, placki z cukinii 19b, knatteplock 18c, lecker 19a (optional).
   - **Salt late or after frying so the mass doesn't weep:** deruny 5c, rårakor 7a.

   All of them name the same mechanism (salt draws liquid).
7. **"Don't turn until the crust has set" is stated as a holding mechanism in four unrelated sources:** rårakor 7a, latkes 4a, Zucchinipuffer 19a and draniki 5a. Several also warn against a cold pan (fat uptake, steaming) or a lid (steam softens the crust, 5c). Frying itself is treated as part of the structure, not only as the finishing step.
8. **Hydrate, then bind.**
   - Semmelknödel 11b soaks the bread in milk + egg, lets it "durchziehen" 15 min, and only then adds flour.
   - Zucchinipuffer 19a lets the flour batter swell 10 min before the squeezed vegetable goes in.
   - Raggmunk 6b rests 30 min, and the Swedish patties 18c "svälla" 5 min.
   - A rest for a dry absorbent to take up water recurs across German, Swedish and Austrian recipes.
9. **Cooling before an egg goes in.** Öko-Test states it for Seidenknödel ("damit das Ei ... nicht stockt"). K-ruoka 17c cools both carrot purée and porridge before adding egg, but gives no reason, so that reading is inference. Pierogi and vareniki fillings are also always used cooled, again with no reason given.
10. **Egg is often optional, and recipes say so.**
    - The Belarusian draniki classic is described as egg- and flour-free (5b, 5c and reader comments in 5a).
    - The MTV porkkanalaatikko is explicitly egg-free (17a), while others use 2 eggs.
    - Kaalilaatikko 14a offers broth *or* egg-milk as a free choice. Finnish cabbage rolls use no egg; Polish gołąbki add one.
    - Ingmar 18b ties egg quantity to equipment: one egg is enough in the oven, two are needed in a pan.
11. **Rice handling for rolls and bakes.** Every Finnish and Polish roll and bake recipe I read used **fully cooked** grain. I found no raw-rice version for these dishes, but I did not search for one exhaustively. The Finnish fillings carry liquid (milk, cream, broth) instead of egg. Karjalanpiirakka uses rice cooked to a thick porridge, cooled or made the day before, and holds by containment: the porridge must not touch the crimp edge (15b).
12. **Treatment matched to the vegetable.** In mixed vegetable patties only the watery vegetable is dewatered: zucchini is squeezed and salted, carrot is not (18c). Martha and Ingmar do not squeeze carrot at all. The Finnish porkkanapihvit (18d) use raw grated potato with no squeezing, in place of breadcrumbs.

## Gaps and caveats (E)
- A Polish raw or half-cooked rice gołąbki recipe was not found or read.
- Ukrainian life.pravda.com.ua (chefs' deruny rules) returned 403. Sveriges Radio raggmunk and ICA raggmunk returned 403. The kwestiasmaku gołąbki URL returned 404. A guessed kotikokki URL served an unrelated recipe (apple-oat bake) and is not cited.
- Several sources are user-submitted recipes (smaker.pl) or SEO-style sites (hurbra.se). They are marked as such.
- The Austria-forum Rösti entry is an encyclopedic mirror, not a recipe.

### M1a. Mawdoo3, "طريقة عمل الطعمية المصرية", Heba El-Tabbaa, upd. 2020 (Arabic)

- Holding: the raw ground bean paste; egg added late (1 per 2 cups paste). Other recipes on the
  same page: one with 2 eggs (blender version), one with no egg but baking soda + 1 h rest + sesame.

### M1b. Mawdoo3, "طريقة عمل الطعمية المصرية مثل المطاعم", Kawthar Abu Omar, upd. 2016 (Arabic)
URL: https://mawdoo3.com/طريقة_عمل_الطعمية_المصرية_مثل_المطاعم

### M1c. Julien Julia blog, "Taameya - Egyptian falafel", 2014 (Arabic, with English version)

3. Optional: portion and freeze the batter.
4. Just before frying: add egg (optional), ground coriander, salt, pepper; re-blend in a blender,

egg (present in some, explicitly optional in 1c, absent in 1b recipe 1). Leavening (soda) is

rest time (none vs 1 h).

## M8. Spinach filo pies: Greek spanakopita vs Turkish ıspanaklı börek

### M12b. Directo al Paladar, "Receta de mi tortilla de patatas", Pintxo, 2008 (Spanish)

## Collector's observations (M)

1. **Raw legume pastes hold without a binder.** Every Arabic-language falafel/ta'ameya recipe
   read (10 recipes on 5 pages) uses raw soaked legumes, never cooked. Most add no egg or flour. Egg
   appears only in some Egyptian fava versions and is marked optional; flour appears only in the
   Sudanese chickpea version. The one stated cohesion control is grind fineness: too coarse
   "won't hold in the oil" (1c). The common English-language explanation "cooked chickpeas fall
   apart" is consistent with this, but the native sources simply never cook them.

2. **Convergence on mechanical dewatering of watery vegetables before binding.** This appears in
   Turkey (mücver, squeeze), Greece (kolokythokeftedes, salt + squeeze; spanakopita, salt-rub +
   squeeze), France (beignets, salt 15 min + squeeze), Sicily (aubergine polpette, weight 30 min +
   squeeze, or sieve) and Italy (parmigiana, salt + weight). Counter-example: the Turkish raw-spinach
   börek (8b) leaves the water in and instead reduces the liquid added to the pastry. Dewatering is
   one way to balance the water, not a universal step.

3. **When frying becomes baking, a dry crumb appears.** Baked kolokythokeftedes (4a) get a
   cornmeal/rusk coat and "enough flour or rusk" for an oil-free version. Baked pastéis de bacalhau
   (14c) switch from whole eggs to yolks + 50 g breadcrumbs, while the fried versions (14a) use whole
   eggs and no crumbs. Two traditions (Greek, Portuguese) independently make the same change.

4. **Cold as a structural step.** Croquetas (chilled gel), arancini/supplì (rice spread to cool,
   overnight allowed), gratin dauphinois (chilled overnight "easier to cut", 15b) and tortilla (rest
   before setting) all rely on time or cold rather than an added ingredient to reach a shapeable or
   sliceable state.

5. **Oil-cooking as dehydration.** Dani García's tortilla (12c) states that the low-temperature
   fry is there to dehydrate the potato, which then "drinks" the egg during a 15-min rest. That is
   the same job salting and squeezing do for courgette, done with heat instead.

6. **Contradictions worth testing at home:**
   - Tortilla: poach without colour (12b) vs fry to light gold (12a, 12c); egg ratio 7.5-15 eggs per
     kg; 12b forbids mashing because starch and egg set "like fried purée".
   - Gemista: raw rice in the oven (7a) vs rice partly cooked first for the covered pot (7b).
   - Gratin: rinse (15a) vs keep slices out of water for their starch (15b).
   - Parmigiana: a Sicilian family egg in the sauce "so it holds when cut" (9b) vs no egg (9a).
   - Croquetas: 13b says the coating barely matters and oil temperature and submersion decide
     whether they burst.

7. **Rice inside a wrapper swells against it.** Turkish sarma toasts raw rice only briefly; Greek
   oven gemista uses raw rice and warns to leave headroom because "the rice swells". The wrapper
   (leaf, vegetable) acts as the mould, and packing tightly or weighting with a plate keeps the
   structure.

8. **Wet tools for sticky masses** recur everywhere: wet spoon for ta'ameya (1c), wet hands for
   arancini and supplì, two wet spoons for pastéis de bacalhau, oiled palms for mücver.

9. **Source independence:** La Comarca (19b) reproduces the Directo al Paladar text (19a). Several
   Mawdoo3 pages share an editorial house style. Three of the karnıyarık recipes come from one
   aggregator (yemek.com). They count as fewer independent witnesses than the raw recipe count.

## A1. Onion pakoda / bhajiya (India, Hindi)

### A1a. Hebbar's Kitchen, अनियन पकोड़ा (Hindi)
URL: https://hebbarskitchen.com/hi/onion-pakoda-recipe-kanda-bhaji/
1. Onion: slice thin into a bowl.

- **Moisture**: the onion's own juice is the only liquid in the batter. Salt goes in with the flour, and squeezing pulls out the juice.

### A1b. Aaj Tak, आलू प्याज भजिया (Hindi)
URL: https://www.aajtak.in/lifestyle/news/story/how-to-make-aloo-pyaz-bhajiya-recipe-at-home-ghar-pe-banayen-aloo-pyaz-bhajiya-tvisp-dskc-2613339-2026-08-01

  - Too tight bursts, too loose falls apart.
  - The double fry keeps it crisp longer.

**Comparison**:
- Three sources agree on egg in the filling, but two of them explicitly **limit** egg as a source of wetness.
- Absorbent additions (taro, potato, breadcrumbs, starch) are used against water.
- Double frying appears in 2 of 3.

## A14. Chiles rellenos capeados (Mexico, Spanish)

### A14a. Excélsior, chiles rellenos de queso capeados (Spanish)
URL: https://www.excelsior.com.mx/recetas/receta-chiles-rellenos-capeados
1. Poblano: char all over on a comal or open flame.
2. Sweat in a closed plastic bag under a damp cloth for 20 min.
3. Peel under running water, then **pat dry**.
4. Slit the side and remove seeds and veins.
5. Stuff with cheese (not overfull). Close with a toothpick.
6. **Pat dry again, dredge in flour and shake off.**
7. Egg foam: beat the whites to firm peaks (they hold when the bowl is inverted). Fold in the yolks one at a time without overbeating. No flour in the batter.
8. Fry in hot oil (170–180 °C) one or two at a time, **basting the top with oil** so the foam sets before turning once.
9. Drain on paper.
- **Stated reasons [source]**:
  - Flour "absorbs moisture and creates a layer so [the egg] sticks" ("absorber la humedad y crear una capa para que se pegue").
  - Seeding reduces heat.
  - Basting stops the batter tearing on the flip.
  - Do not overbeat after the yolks, to keep the air.

### A14b. Univision Delicioso, receta de chiles rellenos (Spanish)
URL: https://www.univision.com/delicioso/receta-de-chiles-rellenos
1. Roast on a comal over medium heat until golden all over.
2. Seal in a plastic bag. Rinse and rub off the skin.
3. Slit and seed. Stuff with about 250 g melting cheese. Close with a toothpick.
4. Holding the chile closed, flour it lightly.
5. Beat **2 whites** until fluffy, then fold in **1 yolk** ("ya no batas, sólo envuelve" = don't beat any more, just fold).
6. Dip and fry immediately on medium-high, both sides.
- **Stated reasons [source]**:
  - Roasting lifts the skin.
  - Trapped steam releases the skin.
  - Holding the chile closed keeps the filling in.
  - No reason is given for the flour.

### A14c. Partial witnesses on the capeado only
- Grupo Animal (https://grupoanimal.mx/gastronomia/recetas/3-trucos-para-capear-chiles-rellenos-a-la-perfeccion-como-los-de-mama):
  - Flour before egg ensures "el huevo no se desprenda" (the egg doesn't come off) during frying.
  - Room-temperature eggs.
  - Some homes add cornstarch to thicken the capeado.
  - 180 °C, or the food absorbs oil.
- Directo al Paladar, Zahie Téllez (https://www.directoalpaladar.com/otros/este-truco-chef-zahie-tellez-para-preparar-claras-hacer-capeado-perfecto): toothpicks keep the filling in, and whites go to punto de turrón.

**Comparison**:
- Four sources agree on two things: dry flour as the interface between a wet peeled chile and the egg foam, and whites whipped to stiff foam with yolks folded in last.
- They disagree on the yolk ratio (one per white in 14a, versus half in 14b) and on whether starch goes in the foam.

## A15. Tamales (Mexico, Spanish)

### A15a. Grupo Animal, masa para tamales desde cero (Spanish)
URL: https://grupoanimal.mx/gastronomia/recetas/masa-para-tamales-desde-cero
1. Husks: soak in hot water.
2. **Beat lard alone** until bright white, like whipped cream or meringue.
3. Add the corn flour and baking powder.
4. Add warm broth gradually to a thick paste that spreads easily with a spoon.
5. **Float test**: drop a small ball of masa into a glass of water. If it sinks, it "needs more air" ("te falta aire"), so beat 5 more min and retest.
6. Steam 60–75 min on medium. Done when the masa comes away from the husk on its own after a 2-minute rest.
- **Stated reasons [source]**: soaking makes the husks flexible so they don't tear. Lard-beating is where most people fail. Runny masa is fixed with more corn flour *while beating*, to avoid lumps.

### A15b. Directo al Paladar, tamales mexicanos paso a paso (Spanish)
URL: https://www.directoalpaladar.com/recetas-de-panes/tamales-mexicanos-como-hacerlos-casa-paso-a-paso
1. Beat room-temperature lard until white and fluffy (a mixer helps).
2. Add Maseca with salt and baking powder and beat.
3. Add warm pork broth little by little ("each flour absorbs differently").
4. Float test as in 15a.
5. Make the filling separately (braised pork in ancho/guajillo adobo).
6. Spread masa on soaked, dried husks. Add filling and fold.
7. Steam about 1.5 h upright on a rack above the water. A coin in the pot rattles when the water runs low.
8. Done when the masa releases easily from the husk.
- **Stated reasons [source]**: the mixer "lets more air in, which is what makes the dough light". The rack keeps the tamales from touching the water.

**Comparison**: the two recipes agree on the essential steps:
- aerate the fat first,
- hydrate the corn gradually,
- prove the aeration with a float test,
- steam until the masa releases from the husk.

Baking powder is in both. Fresh masa versus masa harina is not distinguished in 15a.

## A16. Pão de queijo (Brazil, Portuguese)

### A16a. Receiteria, pão de queijo com polvilho azedo (Portuguese)
URL: https://www.receiteria.com.br/imprimir/?id=366042
1. Sour cassava starch (polvilho azedo): beat with room-temperature water on low until moist, loose pellets form.
2. Boil milk and oil. **Pour the boiling liquid over the starch** ("para escaldar", to scald) and beat 3 min.
3. Add all the eggs and the salt at once. Beat until smooth.
4. Add grated meia-cura cheese and beat about 1 min, just to mix.
5. **Chill** 2 h (or freeze 20 min) until firm enough to roll.
6. Shape balls with buttered hands. Bake at 180 °C for 30 min.

### A16b. TudoGostoso, pão de queijo verdadeiro (Portuguese)
URL: https://www.tudogostoso.com.br/receita/187044-pao-de-queijo-verdadeiro.html
1. Sweet starch (polvilho doce).
2. Boil oil, milk, water and salt. Pour over the starch and stir to scald.
3. Cool about 5 min ("so you don't burn yourself").
4. Add cheese, then eggs **one at a time**, careful not to add too many.
5. Knead until firm enough to roll. Shape with oiled hands. Bake at 180 °C.
- **What holds it (both)**: scalded cassava starch, eggs and cheese. No wheat.
- **Stated reasons**: only "to scald". **[inference]** Scalding pre-gelatinises part of the starch so it forms a cohesive dough. Neither page says so.
- **Comparison**: they disagree on starch type (sour vs sweet), on egg addition (all at once vs one by one, to feel), and on cooling (16b briefly cools; 16a chills afterwards to firm the dough for shaping).

## A18. Akara (West Africa/Nigeria, English) and acarajé (Brazil, Portuguese)

### A18a. Ounje Aladun, "Secret to great akara balls" (English, Nigerian author)
URL: https://ounjealadun.com/2015/03/14/secret-to-great-akara-balls/
1. Beans: peeled (method not described here).
2. Grind to a paste with as little water as possible. The author's mother took two bowls to the mill to control the water.
3. **Aerate**: traditionally pound in a mortar until it doubles. With a mixer, whisk 10–15 min until doubled.
4. Blend onion and pepper separately and season. **Fold into the fluffy paste in three additions**, like a sponge cake, so it does not deflate. Salt goes in last, then stop mixing.
5. Deep-fry in hot oil (a water drop sizzles, and the oil is ready when the sizzle stops).
- **What holds it**: nothing added. The author tried eggs and dropped them; the egg-free method "has never failed me".
- **Stated reasons [source]**:
  - "Never add too much water in grinding". A runny paste gives flat akara.
  - Air makes the balls light.
  - A watery paste over-whisked turns spongy and soaks oil.
  - Deep frying gives better balls.

### A18b. Chef Lola's Kitchen, Akara/Acarajé (English, Nigerian author)
URL: https://cheflolaskitchen.com/akara-acaraje/
1. Black-eyed peas: soak 15–30 min until the skins swell.
2. Pulse a few times to loosen the skins. Swirl in water and skim off the floating skins. Repeat.
3. Blend with about ¼ cup water, pepper, habanero, salt, bouillon and crayfish.
4. Whisk 3–4 min by hand (1–2 min with a mixer) until light and fluffy.
5. Fold in diced onion gently, in one direction.
6. Drop spoonfuls into hot oil (medium-high), in batches. Fry 5–6 min, turning. Press gently before lifting to release oil.
- **What holds it**: nothing added (no egg or flour).
- **Stated reasons [source]**:
  - Too much water makes a thin batter that **crumbles in the oil**.
  - Blending too smooth gives dense akara.
  - Flat akara means not enough air.

### A18c. TudoGostoso, "Como fazer acarajé" (Portuguese, Bahia)
URL: https://www.tudogostoso.com.br/noticias/como-fazer-acaraje-a1506.htm
1. Black-eyed peas (feijão-fradinho): crack lightly in a blender, then soak about 1.5 h "to loosen the skin and black specks".
2. Change the water repeatedly, removing the skins. Drain.
3. Blend with onion and salt, adding water only if needed.
4. Beat well with a wooden spoon until very soft.
5. Fry spoonfuls in well-heated dendê (palm oil). Drain.
- **What holds it**: nothing added.

**Comparison**: all three follow one sequence:
1. Skin the beans.
2. Grind with minimal water.
3. Beat or whisk.
4. Spoon into deep hot oil.

None adds a binder. They disagree on soak time (15 min to 1.5 h) and on whether onion and salt go into the grind or are folded in after aeration (18a explicitly folds in late to protect the foam). **[inference]** Acarajé is historically descended from West African akara, so 18c is diffusion, not independent convergence.

## A19. Moin moin / moi moi (Nigeria, English)

### A19a. Chef Lola's Kitchen, moin moin (English)
URL: https://cheflolaskitchen.com/make-moin-moin-moi-moi/
1. Beans: soak about 15 min in **room-temperature** water (not hot).
2. Pulse-blend in bursts to loosen the skins. Rub and float them off, then sieve.
3. Blend peeled beans with red pepper, chilli, onion, ginger, crayfish, bouillon and salt, with about 1 cup water, until very smooth.
4. Stir in about ¼ cup oil (vegetable or palm).
5. Fold in optional cooked fish or liver. Pour into greased leaves or ramekins and set a boiled egg on top.
6. Steam 45–60 min on medium, topping up the water.
7. Done when a knife comes out fairly clean and the pudding is moist but firm.
- **What holds it**: the bean paste sets on steaming. No starch or flour. Egg is a garnish, not a binder.
- **Stated reasons [source]**:
  - Hot soaking water part-cooks the beans and spoils the texture.
  - Under-blending gives a grainy pudding.
  - Too much water "wouldn't mold well".
  - **Tomato makes it difficult to solidify.**
  - No reason is given for the oil.

### A19b. Kitchen Butterfly, Moin Moin (English, Nigerian author)
URL: https://kitchenbutterfly.com/2010/moin-moin-steamed-nigerian-bean-cake
1. Skinned black-eyed beans (skinning in a separate post).
2. Blend with onion, chilli and **stock (no water)** to smooth and thick.
3. Salt to taste ("of the sea"). Stir in ¼–½ cup oil.
4. Target consistency like shop-bought yoghurt.
5. Fill oiled ramekins ¾ full, or wrap in banana leaf, corn husk or foil tied with leaf strips.
6. Steam about 30 min. Done when firm to the touch with no wet residue.
- **What holds it**: the paste alone. The wraps hold the shape.

**Comparison**: same raw paste as akara (skinned, ground beans), but **not aerated** and with **oil added**, then steam-set in a mould instead of fried. Both recipes keep the paste pourable but not watery.

## Collector's observations (A)

**Convergences**
1. **Soaked raw pulse paste, no binder (India, Nigeria, Brazil; and Egypt from the brief).** Medu vada (urad), akara and acarajé (black-eyed pea) follow the same sequence: soak, grind with minimal water, aerate mechanically, spoon into deep oil. None adds egg or flour. Rice flour in 3a is for crispness.
   - Every source names the same failure: too much water gives flat, oily or crumbling results (3a, 18a, 18b, 19a).
   - Acarajé is a descendant of akara (diffusion). Urad vada vs akara is genuinely independent.
   - Moi moi takes the same paste down a different route: no aeration, oil added, steamed in a mould.
2. **Aeration by beating, checked by flotation.**
   - Tamale masa (lard beaten first, float test in both recipes).
   - Medu vada and akara (whipped paste; akara "doubles").
   - Capeado (egg-white foam with yolks folded in).
   - Okonomiyaki (fold to keep air, don't press, 7b/7c).
   - Akara 18a and capeado both add other ingredients **last, by folding**, to protect the foam. These are independent traditions solving "keep the air in a heavy paste".
3. **Dry flour or starch layer between a wet surface and a coating.** Found in:
   - tempura uchiko (4a),
   - kakiage flour dusting (5a),
   - chile relleno flour before the egg foam (14a, 14b, Grupo Animal),
   - hobak-jeon flour before egg (9a),
   - qiehe starch inside the pocket (12a),
   - nem sealed with starch paste (13b).

   Japan, Mexico, Korea and China state it in nearly the same words: it absorbs surface moisture and gives the coating something to grip. One nuance: 4a says dusting makes the batter cling *less* (thinner coat), while 5a says it makes batter stick *more*.
4. **Salt and wait, then decide what to do with the water.** The salting step is shared across Hindi, Korean, Chinese and Brazilian sources, but the use of the liquid splits:
   - Water **becomes the batter liquid**, with "no water added" said explicitly in both: onion pakoda 1a, shredded hobak-jeon 9b.
   - Water is **removed**: courgette dumpling/bing 11a/11b, hobak-jeon slices 9a, eggplant 12b.
   - Water is **prevented** by delaying salt: bhajiya 1b, final salt in dumpling 11a, medu vada salt just before frying 3a.

   Delaying salt (or wet vegetables) until the last moment appears in India (1b, 3a), China (11a) and Japan (okonomiyaki cabbage, 7a).
5. **Absorb instead of remove.** Several sources add a dry absorbent rather than (or as well as) squeezing:
   - fried crushed vermicelli in courgette bing (11b, stated "absorbs moisture"),
   - taro, potato, breadcrumbs or potato starch in nem (13b, stated),
   - poha powder or rice flour in aloo tikki (2a, stated),
   - extra flour until not runny in bolinho 17b.
6. **Double frying for crusts over wet interiors.** Independently in Vietnam (13b, 13c), Chinese eggplant boxes (12b) and one medu vada (3b). The stated reason is that moisture from inside softens the crust, and a second high-heat fry keeps it crisp.

**Contradictions between recipes of the same dish**
- Hobak-jeon: the slice + flour + egg structure vs a shred batter with only salt-drawn water and no egg (9a vs 9b).
- Gamja-jeon: recover the potato's own settled starch vs discard the water and add bought starch (10a vs 10b).
- Bolinho de abobrinha: squeeze all the water out vs don't squeeze and add flour (17a vs 17b).
- Medu vada: 3 h soak, warning against longer, with beating essential, vs overnight soak, no beating, double fry (3a vs 3b).
- Qiehe: starch glue, egg-starch coat and one fry vs salted eggplant, flour/ice-water batter and two fries (12a vs 12b).
- Okonomiyaki: lid vs no lid. Egg in the batter vs folded in with the solids.
- Pão de queijo: sour vs sweet starch, eggs all at once vs one by one.

**Surprises / against common assumptions**
- **Egg as a source of wetness, not only a binder.** Both Vietnamese nem sources cap the egg (one per 300–400 g filling, or one per 20–25 rolls) because more egg makes the filling wet and the wrapper soggy or burst. The akara author tested egg and rejected it. The shredded hobak-jeon has no egg (only reviewers added it).
- **Neither tempura recipe read uses whole egg in the batter.** One replaces it with mayonnaise and says egg's job includes suppressing gluten. The other uses potato starch and ice water.
- **Korokke bursting is explained as steam.** Moisture in the filling expands before the crust sets. The remedies are boiling the potato whole in its skin, drying the meat, cooling the filling and not crowding the oil. The structure fails from inside, not from a weak binder.
- **Tomato prevents moi moi from setting** (19a). This is a specific stated incompatibility in a pulse-protein set.
- **Hebbar's onion pakoda adds salt together with the flour**, not to the onion first, yet still relies on squeezing out the onion's juice. Aaj Tak delays salt for the opposite reason. "Salt the onion first" is not universal in the Hindi sources read.
